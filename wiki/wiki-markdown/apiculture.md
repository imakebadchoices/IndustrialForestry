# Apiculture — Beekeeping

> *"Some people ask me why I bother with bees. I've been burned, poisoned and blown up countless times, and once they even devoured my home. But although I am now covered in scars, I have been rewarded with gifts beyond my wildest dreams."*
> — Sir Sengir, from the remains of *Beekeeping 101*

Beekeeping is Forestry's oldest system and the usual first step into the mod. You catch wild bees from the hives that dot the world, house them so they work and multiply, and breed them across dozens of species toward rarer bees with better products and stranger habits. Almost everything else in Forestry is downstream of a bee: combs and drops are the raw feedstock the [Centrifuge](centrifuge.md) and other machines refine, and the honey they yield ends up powering the [engines](energy.md).

This page is the overview. The details of where bees live and how you work them are split across two companion pages: **[Bee Housing](bee-housing.md)** (the Bee House, Apiary, Alveary and frames) and **[Beekeeping Tools](bee-tools.md)** (the Scoop, Beealyzer, Smoker and Apiarist's armour).

## Finding your first bees

<figure class="illus-right"><img src="images/hive.png" alt="A wild bee hive"><figcaption>A wild hive</figcaption></figure>

Bees live in **wild hives** that generate throughout the world, and the hive tells you what climate the bee inside prefers. Hives glow faintly as the bees inside work, so the easiest way to find them is to explore at night — though the same glow tends to attract other, less friendly things.

To get the bees out you need a **[Scoop](bee-tools.md)**. Beat on a hive with it and it breaks cleanly, dropping a **Princess** and one or more **Drones**; hit it with anything else and you destroy the bee along with the hive. A Princess and a single Drone are all you need to begin.

Each hive generates in a biome that matches its bee's tastes:

| Hive | Bee | Typically found in |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | Forest | Forests and wooded hills |
| Meadows | Meadows | Plains and open grassland |
| Desert | Modest | Deserts — hot and arid |
| Jungle | Tropical | Jungles and other humid biomes |
| Swamp | Marshy | Swamps — damp and cool |
| Snow | Wintry | Snowy, frozen biomes |
| Savanna | Savanna | Savannas |
| Lush | Lush | Lush caves |
| Aquatic | Aquatic | In and around water |
| Nether | Embittered | The Nether |
| End | Ended | The End |

The **Valiant** bee is the odd one out: it has no hive of its own, but occasionally turns up living alongside the bees in an ordinary hive. Together with the overworld hive bees it forms the root stock every other line is bred from.

## Breeding and genetics

<figure class="illus-right"><img src="images/bee.png" alt="A Forestry bee"><figcaption>Princess, Drone, or Queen</figcaption></figure>

Placing a Princess and a Drone together in an [Apiary or Alveary](bee-housing.md) mates them into a **Queen**. The Queen works through a fixed lifespan producing combs, then dies — leaving behind a fresh Princess and several Drones. That death-and-renewal *is* the reproduction step, and it makes bee populations grow quickly.

Three rules govern everything that follows:

- **Every bee carries two alleles for each trait**, one from each parent, and one is dominant. A trait you want can ride along recessively for many generations without showing, which is exactly what makes drones worth hoarding — they are a genetic library. A **[Beealyzer](bee-tools.md)** is the only way to read those hidden alleles.

- **Climate matters.** Every species has a preferred temperature and humidity. Work a bee outside its range and its output suffers, unless an Alveary's climate components compensate.

- **Many mutations are conditional.** Crossing the right two parents is often necessary but not sufficient — some new species only appear underground, in the Nether, at night, or under other specific conditions.

## The breeding ladder

Two mutations open up the entire tree, and both are worked from the ten root **hive bees** (Forest, Marshy, Meadows, Modest, Savanna, Tropical, Valiant, Wintry, Lush, and Aquatic):

- **Common** — cross any two *different* hive bees (base **15%** chance). It is unremarkable in itself, but the gateway to the rest of the tree.
- **Cultivated** — cross a Common with any hive bee (base **12%**). It is fast and productive, and it parents most of the noble and industrious lines.

From Common and Cultivated the tree splits into two classic branches:

- The **noble** line — Noble → Majestic → Imperial — trends toward long-lived royalty and the rich royal jelly the Alveary craves.
- The **industrious** line — Diligent → Unweary → Industrious — trends toward relentless speed, and the Industrious bee brings back pollen as it works.

Beyond these lie the genus families that end in prized specialists — icy, infernal, metallic, and stranger still.

## Products

Combs and drops are raw materials for downstream processing. **Combs** go into a **Centrifuge**, which breaks them down into beeswax, honey drops, and the occasional specialty — propolis, pollen, honeydew, and rarer drops from exotic bees. **Honey drops** go into a **Squeezer** to press out honey fluid, which feeds the **Fermenter** and **Still** on the way to biofuel. Refined further, bee products also yield **royal jelly**, **ambrosia**, and pollen used in the Alveary and in crafting. See the [Centrifuge](centrifuge.md) and [Squeezer](squeezer.md) for the processing chain and [Engines](energy.md) for where the fuels end up.

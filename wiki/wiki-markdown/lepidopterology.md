# Lepidopterology — Butterflies

<img class="splash" src="images/butterfly.jpg" alt="Forestry butterflies">

Lepidopterology is the third genetics branch, and the most ornamental. You catch, breed, and mutate butterflies much as you do bees and trees, but the payoff leans toward collection, decoration, pollination, and a handful of caterpillar products rather than an industrial supply chain. If you have learned the bee system, the mental model carries over directly.

## Catching butterflies

Wild butterflies flutter around the world in daylight, favouring biomes that suit their climate. To catch one, right-click it with a **[Scoop](bee-tools.md)** — the same tool you use on bee hives — and it drops as an item carrying its genome. From there it behaves like any other Forestry specimen: analyse it, breed it, or release it to flit around your base.

## The life cycle

Butterflies reproduce through a full metamorphosis rather than a queen-and-drone cycle:

1. A mated butterfly lays **caterpillars** on suitable leaves.
2. Each caterpillar feeds and matures, then spins a **cocoon**.
3. The cocoon hatches into an adult butterfly — some of which carry **mutated** genomes toward new species.

Breeding two different species can therefore yield a third, following fixed mutation recipes exactly as bees and trees do. The genome is read and manipulated with the same analyser tools, and butterfly genetics can also be extracted into **serums**.

## Butterflies at work

Butterflies are not purely decorative. As they rest on leaves they **pollinate trees**, which makes a population of loose butterflies a passive aid to [tree breeding](arboriculture.md). Their **caterpillars** are also a minor product in their own right.

## Rarity and climate

Each species carries a **rarity weight** that shapes how readily it appears and breeds — lower weights mean rarer, harder-won butterflies — and, like every Forestry organism, a preferred temperature and humidity that decides which biomes it thrives in.

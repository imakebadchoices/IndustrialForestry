# Arboriculture — Tree Breeding

> *"Generations of foresters have wondered at the noticeable lack of diversity in Minecraftia's tree species… due to the demands of builders everywhere, foresters have taken the breeding successes of beekeepers as cause to dabble into treebreeding."*
> — foreword to the *Forester's Almanac*

Arboriculture runs the same genetics engine as beekeeping, applied to trees. Instead of queens and drones you work with **saplings** and **pollen**, and instead of combs you're after specialty **woods** and **fruits**. It is a slower craft — trees take real time to grow — but it produces materials you cannot easily get elsewhere, and it pairs naturally with the [Multifarm](farming.md) for automated logging.

## Getting started

Every tree species has a **sapling** that carries its full genome, exactly as a bee carries its own. Your starting stock is the ordinary set of species — the vanilla-equivalent Oak, Birch, Spruce and so on — which you plant, let grow, and harvest for wood and, on fruiting species, for fruit. A **[Portable Analyzer](bee-tools.md)** reads a sapling's genome just as a Beealyzer reads a bee's: its species, how many saplings it yields, how fast it grows, how tall, the girth of its trunk, its fruit type and yield, and its preferred climate.

## Pollination and breeding

Trees do not breed by being placed side by side — they breed by **pollination**, and the pollinators are your bees. Almost any bee will cross-pollinate nearby trees; a faster, well-bred bee simply does it sooner. Butterflies carry pollen too.

The method is simple:

1. Plant two **different** tree species close together.
2. Set an **Apiary** (or Alveary) nearby and keep a working queen in it, so bees are active in the area.
3. After a while — depending on the bee and how dense the surrounding foliage is — some leaves will **change colour**. Those leaves have been pollinated by a tree of different genetics.
4. Break the coloured leaves and hope for a sapling. Some of those saplings carry a **mutated** genome toward a new species.
5. Replant and repeat, laddering toward the wood or fruit you want.

### The Grafter

Breaking leaves by hand often drops nothing. The **Grafter** fixes that: use it on leaves and you are almost guaranteed to extract a sapling, including the pollinated ones that carry mutations. The basic Grafter is fragile (good for a handful of uses); the **Proven Grafter** is far sturdier and lasts many times longer, and is the tool to carry once you are breeding seriously.

## Woods

The main payoff of arboriculture is access to many **wood types** beyond the vanilla few, each with its own logs, planks, and decorative blocks (stairs, slabs, fences, doors, and more). Forestry woods can also be made **fireproof**, a genuinely useful property for building near lava or in the Nether. These woods feed the [Carpenter](carpenter.md) and ordinary building recipes.

## Charcoal & fruit

Beyond wood, arboriculture supports a **charcoal** production path — stacking logs into a [Wood Pile](charcoal.md) and charring them at scale — and, on fruiting species, a supply of **fruits** that can be pressed for juice and seed oil in the [Squeezer](squeezer.md).
